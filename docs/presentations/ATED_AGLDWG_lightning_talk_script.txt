    Australian Thesaurus of Education Descriptors or ATED has been core Australian education terminology since 1979. ACER approached KurrawongAI with a MultiTes XML export. The XML was structurally consistent, but it did not carry a published Semantic Web model: relationships were expressed through labels, non-descriptors pointed to preferred terms using old fashioned "USE", and the durable term numbers were buried in the MultiTes application.

So the modelling was intentionally conservative. Descriptor records became SKOS concepts. The MultiTes term number became both the stable IRI suffix and the SKOS notation. USE terms became alternative labels, and broader, narrower and related-term links became ordinary SKOS relationships. Subject categories remain a small, separate scheme. The result is 5,280 concepts and 41 categories.

Subject categories for what are essentially subjects is not my favourite thing in vocab practices. Nevertheless, we applied the same VocPub standards to the categories so that they indicated provenance, were versionable and modify-able, and publication ready.

ATED concepts have scope notes but do not have definitions - many vocabularies do not. VocPub compliance was a goal in the modelling, but strict requirements for documentation properties can have perverse consequences. A boilerplate approach was used: "[X] is a concept in the Australian Thesaurus of Education Descriptors" features throughout. At a glance, such definitions are redundant - but at least concepts discovered out of context have human-readable information about a source concept scheme and hints at the creator / publisher.

The surprisingly interesting part was not ontology design; it was making the conversion repeatable and the publication usable. We separated Python script from mapping rules into the more declarative YAML, kept static scheme metadata in Turtle, generated the graph with RDFLib, and validate it with VocPub SHACL validator. That makes the transformation inspectable and repeatable rather than a one-off script. The source also exposed practical edge cases: resolving label-based links safely, preserving identifiers, and making duplicate search results understandable when a concept matches through more than one label.

The result is now visible in KurrawongAI demo Prez system, giving people a browsable vocabulary and machines RDF and SPARL access. ACER also receives the self-contained RDF file, so the asset is not locked to a hosting choice. The next-stage options range from file handover, through a shared managed Prez and K-Maker service, to dedicated or client-hosted deployment. The key point is that the identifiers and data remain stable even when the hosting model changes.
